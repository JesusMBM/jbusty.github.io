07 / AI SECURITY REVIEW

They name the trap. Then they open it.

Horizon3.ai asked whether human cyber-deception assumptions transfer to LLM attackers. Same recon instrument. Different attacker class. This is the walkthrough.

Back to the portfolio →
+

What it is

A recon quiz, not a live agent.

Honeyquest, from Kahlhofer et al. at RAID 2024, is a questionnaire dressed as recon. You get numbered artifact lines. Each line is labeled neutral, risky, or deceptive. You mark exploit if you would probe it, trap if it looks like bait, or you leave it alone.

Horizon3.ai ran that instrument on LLMs. Each model returned exploit marks, trap marks, and a short justification. That is the cognitive layer. Marks plus reasoning. It is not a full agent harness. No tools, no memory, no multi-turn loop.

The study used 21 models across 10 providers on the original 174 recon queries. Three independent trials per model and query. That produced 10,962 LLM answers, scored against the 47-person Kahlhofer baseline.

Treat 78.5 and 73.4 as judgment numbers. They are not what a tool-using agent does after it has a shell.

Finding 1. Enticement

Models take the bait more than twice as often.

The LLM cohort fell for traps in 78.5% of answers. Humans did it 37% of the time. All 21 models sat above the human 95% confidence interval. Floor 61% on Kimi K2-thinking. Ceiling 92.5% on Sonnet 4.6.

They also hit real risks more. Risk detected 77.6% versus 44%. Trap detection stayed close, 13.5% versus 15%. The models are greedy, not blind.

Finding 2. No diversion

The defender benefit that works on people does not show up here.

Honeyquest's original claim is diversion. Plant bait, and humans mark the real risk less. Humans moved ΔRR −22% (p=0.0013). Deception reduced risk-marking.

The LLM cohort moved the other way. ΔRR +9.9%, not significant. You cannot conclude diversion. Models still see the real hole, and they still mark the bait. The cost of doing both is tokens, not courage.

One paired technique did pull models off the real risk. httpheaders-devtoken, ΔRR −11.3%. The rest of the paired set did not.

Finding 3. Recognition-action gap

Naming the trap is not avoiding it.

When a model said trap-shaped things in its reasoning, it still exploit-marked the bait 73.4% of the time. That is walked-into-trap. It bound that recognition to a trap mark only 20.1% of the time. Side-stepped is the rare move.

Recognition versus fell-for-trap does not track. Spearman r=+0.08, p=0.73. Naming the honeyfile in the justification is not a refusal. Assertive style does track falling for traps, r=+0.54, p=0.012. Confidence on the page, bait in the marks.

What to do

If the attacker is a model, cost is tokens, not fear.

People get diverted. An LLM attacker does not owe you that psychology. AI-native deception is a token and context cost, not a human-psych trick. Bait that burns budget and writes a log is the useful part.

Do not bet that an obvious passwords.txt will pull the model off config.php. Human-psych assumptions (divert, scare, obvious honeyfile) do not transfer cleanly. Build deception that wastes tokens. Stop waiting for the model to flinch.
