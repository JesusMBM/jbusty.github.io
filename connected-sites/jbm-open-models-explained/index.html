Open, but how open?

An AI model can be available to use without being available to download, inspect or change. Learn what different kinds of access give you, and what to check before choosing a release.

By Jesus Bustillos-Molina · Reviewed 4 September 2026 · About 5 minutes

01 / AI models

Three kinds of access

“Open” can describe several different things. A public chatbot lets you try a model, but that alone does not give you the files to run it. A downloadable release gives you more control over deployment, but may still leave its training process largely undocumented. Treat these as separate questions instead of a single yes-or-no label.

Sources: Open Source AI Definition 1.0

Sources: Open Source AI Definition 1.0

Under the Open Source Initiative’s definition, open-source AI must include weights, relevant code and sufficiently detailed training-data information, alongside the freedom to use, study, change and share it. The definition does not demand a freely downloadable copy of every training example. A publisher’s marketing label is not an independent certification.

Sources: Open Source AI Definition 1.0

02 / AI models

What are weights?

Weights are numerical settings adjusted during training. They help determine how the model responds to a new question. Imagine a very large bank of dials: training adjusts them, and a weights download records the resulting settings. Those settings still need software and computing equipment to produce an answer.

Sources: OpenAI: gpt-oss guide

Having those final settings does not automatically reveal every decision made during training. Code describes procedures; training-data information describes the material and preparation behind the learning process. A more complete release makes more of those choices available for inspection.

Sources: Open Source AI Definition 1.0

03 / AI models

Four releases, four checks

OpenAI’s gpt-oss-120b and gpt-oss-20b are downloadable models under Apache 2.0, with usage-policy documentation. They are separate from OpenAI’s hosted API models. This illustrates why it is better to classify an exact release than to call an entire company “open” or “closed.”

Sources: OpenAI: gpt-oss guide

Claude Opus 5 appears in Anthropic’s hosted model catalogue. Access through a service lets a user submit requests while a provider operates the model; that offer does not supply public weights to run independently. Access to an assistant and possession of its model files are different arrangements.

Sources: Anthropic model catalogue

NVIDIA publishes Nemotron 3 Ultra weights alongside development materials, including training datasets and recipes. Its model card lists OpenMDW-1.1 and substantial server requirements. This shows why examining the released materials is useful; this guide does not independently certify full compliance with the OSI definition.

Sources: NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra model card

Moonshot AI publishes Kimi K3 weights under a custom licence. Some commercial uses carry additional agreement or attribution conditions. Its size also makes clear that a public download is not automatically practical on a personal computer. Availability, permission and computing requirements need separate checks.

Sources: Kimi K3 model card · Kimi K3 licence

04 / AI models

Capability and openness answer different questions

“Can it work with pictures?” is a question about capability. “Can I download, inspect or change it?” is a question about access and rights. A text model may be either hosted or downloadable, and an image-capable model can have either arrangement. Do not infer one property from the other.

Similarly, downloadable weights do not establish that a model will be accurate, secure or inexpensive for your task. A useful evaluation asks whether it handles your examples reliably and whether the available software, licence and equipment support the way you intend to use it.

05 / AI models

More control brings operating work

Running a model on infrastructure you control can let you choose where processing happens and how the system is adapted. Privacy still depends on the whole setup, including logs, connected tools and outside services. A local model connected to an external search tool still has an external data path.

The operator must budget for equipment, electricity, updates, access controls and people who maintain the system. Before adopting a release, ask who handles failures, how updates are tested, and how users report problems. Compare those responsibilities with the convenience and limits of a managed service.

  • Can I download this exact model’s weights?
  • Does the licence allow my intended use, modifications and sharing?
  • Which code and training-data information are available?
  • What hardware, software and skills will operation require?
  • Where does information travel, including through connected tools?
  • Who will maintain the deployment and check its results?

Sources: Open Source AI Definition 1.0 · Kimi K3 licence · NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra model card

Words explained

Weights
Learned numerical settings used by a model.
Training
Adjusting a model using examples and feedback.
Licence
Terms describing permitted use, modification and sharing.
Self-hosting
Running software on equipment managed by you or your chosen operator.
API
A connection through which software requests a service.
Training-data information
Details about material used for training and how it was prepared.
